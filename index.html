    About Me

    Quality.

    Quality isn’t something we test in. It’s something we build in.

    To me, quality isn’t a checklist. It’s a mindset: the standards I hold myself to, the way I treat people, and the choices I make when no one’s checking my work. That mindset followed me into software testing, and eventually became a career. I don’t see QA as just finding bugs. It’s understanding how something is supposed to work, questioning what could go wrong, and helping the people building it make it better.

    Roots

    That perspective was shaped long before I got into technology. I was born and raised in Utah, carrying both Filipino and British heritage. My mother immigrated to the U.S. from the Philippines with little more than grit and gratitude. My father, a psychologist who later reinvented himself as a software developer, passed away in 2014 from multiple myeloma. Watching the people around me adapt to hard circumstances taught me something I still carry: life doesn’t always give you control over what happens, but you always have control over what you do next. That’s shaped who I am, and how I approach the work.

    Utah, and the Questions I Keep Asking

    I’ve always been curious about how things work. Whether I’m building something, fixing something, or taking something apart, I tend to ask the same question: “Why does it work this way?”

    That mindset has followed me into QA. I don’t just want to know that something works. I want to understand why it works, how it behaves when things don’t go as planned, and what we can do to make it better. I think that curiosity is one of the most important parts of quality.

    The People Who Shaped Me

    My grandmother was one of the people I admired most: sharp intelligence paired with hard-earned wisdom, the kind that comes from living through hard chapters without letting them define you.

    Learn, Earn, Return

    “There are three parts to your life. One part you learn, the other part you earn, the next part you return.”

    / Denzel Washington

    I try to live in all three at once, not take them in turns. I’m still learning constantly: new tools, new AI-assisted workflows, new ways to think about quality. I earn through the work itself. And I return it by mentoring the next wave of QA engineers, many of whom I met back when I was still figuring this out myself. Doing all three at the same time, instead of waiting your turn for each one, is what actually opens doors of opportunity.

    Habits I Try to Live By

    Stephen Covey’s The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People is a framework I keep coming back to, not because I’ve mastered it, but because it’s a good measuring stick:

    1. Be Proactive
    2. Begin with the End in Mind
    3. Put First Things First
    4. Think Win-Win
    5. Seek First to Understand, Then to Be Understood
    6. Synergize
    7. Sharpen the Saw

    A few of these show up directly in how I work: seeking to understand before being understood is half of what makes someone good at QA, thinking win-win is what makes cross-team collaboration actually hold up, and sharpening the saw is why I’m still learning new tools years into this career instead of coasting on what I already know.

    If I had to sum up how I try to live, it’s a phrase I’ve carried for years: “Manners maketh man.” Quality isn’t just about the work. It’s about how you treat people along the way.
